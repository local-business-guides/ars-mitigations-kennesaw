A homeowner's guide to local mold remediation

Arranging local mold remediation is easier when you know what information to share. A few notes about water history and room access can help you prepare for a useful first conversation.

Starting your local mold remediation request

When you contact ARS Mitigations in Kennesaw, describe the location of your concern as specifically as you can. Saying it's beneath the upstairs bathroom or along the garage ceiling gives more context than saying there's mold somewhere in the house.

Explain whether water is still entering the space. Mention a recent appliance leak, roof problem or plumbing repair if you know about one. You don't need to identify the type of mold or figure out the cleanup method before calling.

Share what you've observed without guessing about hidden damage. Dates, room names and changes you've noticed are useful starting points for a conversation about your property.

Before a local mold remediation visit

Prepare the route to the affected space. Tell the company about locked gates, attic access, tight crawlspace openings or furniture that blocks an entry. Ask before moving anything that appears affected, since carrying it through the house could disturb material.

Keep children and pets away from the area you're concerned about. Avoid brushing, sanding or pulling apart suspect surfaces while waiting for guidance. There's no need to expose more of the wall yourself to make the visit worthwhile.

Have a short checklist ready:

  • Note the room and the nearest plumbing or exterior opening.
  • Write down when any water incident occurred.
  • Collect records of related repairs, if available.
  • Identify items stored against the affected surface.
  • Explain any limits on access or appointment timing.
  • List questions about using nearby rooms during work.

If you're a tenant, notify the property owner or manager and clarify who can authorize work. If you own the home but someone else will meet the contractor, make sure that person has your notes.

When to request local mold remediation help

Professional help is worth discussing when the concern involves wall cavities, flooring layers, insulation or other materials you can't easily assess. The same applies when discoloration returns after cleaning or follows a water event that affected several parts of a room.

A musty odor without a visible source is another reason to ask about an evaluation. Smell alone doesn't establish what is present, so describe it without treating it as a diagnosis. Let the visit focus on understanding the conditions rather than confirming an assumption.

ARS Mitigations lists 24/7 availability. When calling outside regular daytime hours, explain any active water problem and ask what response is appropriate for the situation.

Keeping local mold cleanup organized

Once work is proposed, decide who will receive updates and approve changes. One household contact can help keep instructions consistent, particularly when family members have different schedules. Ask how unexpected findings will be communicated before additional work begins.

Discuss what you can use while the job is underway. A hallway, bathroom or storage room may affect daily routines even when it's only a small part of the house. Get instructions for entering or avoiding the work space directly from the company.

Save the work description and any completion records with your home maintenance files. If a separate repair is needed to stop water entry, keep track of that appointment too. Cleanup planning and moisture repairs need to stay coordinated.

Questions people ask about local mold remediation

What should I tell a local mold remediation company when I call?

Give the room location, describe what you've noticed and explain any known water incident. Also mention access restrictions and whether water is still entering the property.

Do I need to move everything before the visit?

Ask for instructions before moving belongings near the affected area. Describe anything blocking access so the company can help you plan without unnecessarily disturbing materials.

Can I arrange local mold remediation for a rental home?

Notify your landlord or property manager and establish who is authorized to approve the work. You can help by documenting your observations and providing access information.
